# R17.18. What the sandbox doesn't do

R17.18. **What the sandbox doesn't do.** It doesn't use the **Wayland security-context** protocol,
which would close the wlroots leak and KDE's clipboard leak (R17.9); sway since wlroots 0.17 and kwin 6
advertise it, as the `wayland` and `kwin6` harnesses show. It has no **Landlock** layer as
defense-in-depth inside bwrap (execute-deny, per-port TCP). There are no **personal templates** and no
**reviewer-defined or shared templates** (R17.3.4), and enabling a sandbox later doesn't **import the
app's configuration** (R17.13). Its **seccomp filter** is the terminal-injection one of R17.4, not a
fuller one (Flatpak's also refuses `ptrace` of other sandboxes, `perf_event_open` and unusual socket
families). An AppImage is unpacked, not mounted with **squashfuse** (R17.7). Proprietary-**NVIDIA**
device nodes aren't bound, and **play-only sound** isn't enforced (R17.8). There is no
**capability-stripping AppArmor profile** for bwrap's children on 24.04 (R17.10.1), and no **spawner
for Chromium's and Electron's own sandbox** on Ubuntu 25.04 and later (R17.10.2: as Flatpak's zypak
does, a host-side helper per launch that would start each sandboxed Chromium child in its own,
tighter bwrap, so the app's inner sandbox would come back instead of `--no-sandbox`). An app has no
network of its own (**user-mode networking**, `pasta` or `slirp4netns`, which would keep the host's
abstract sockets out for every session; R17.9; review 3, R3-8), and no machine-id of its own with
`/sys` narrowed to what GPU drivers need (R17.17; review 3, R3-7). There is no filtered **AT-SPI
proxy**, as Flatpak runs, for screen readers to reach sandboxed apps (R17.17; review 2, F15). A file
still to be made isn't handed over as a name in its folder (the document portal's `AddNamed`,
R17.6.2; review 3, R3-16). **App categories** in the Catalog are a separate feature, not part of the
sandbox. What only a real desktop shows is the checklist in `local-proxy-test-plan.md`, run by
`scripts/sandbox/field-test.sh` against a probe app AppKata itself installs, in a throwaway home.
